4. DFPLAYER MINI WIRING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  DFPlayer VCC  ---> Arduino 5V
  DFPlayer GND  ---> Common ground
  DFPlayer TX   ---> Arduino D2 (direct)
  DFPlayer RX   ---> Voltage divider from Arduino D3:

      Arduino D3 ---- 680 ohm ----+---- DFPlayer RX
                                  |
                                 1k ohm
                                  |
                                 GND

      Output: 5V x (1000 / (680 + 1000)) = ~2.98V (safe for 3.3V RX)

  DFPlayer SPK1 ---> Speaker wire 1
  DFPlayer SPK2 ---> Speaker wire 2

  microSD card:
  - FAT32 format
  - folder: /mp3
  - file: /mp3/0001.mp3
  - 44.1kHz MP3 recommended


5. SENSORS AND LEDs
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  HC-SR501 PIR SENSOR (white Fresnel dome on green PCB):
  - VCC ---> Arduino 5V
  - OUT ---> Arduino D7
  - GND ---> Common ground
  - Two orange potentiometers: sensitivity + delay adjustment
  - 60-second warm-up period enforced in firmware after power-on

  North Star LED (orange, on backdrop):
  - Arduino D4 ---> 220 ohm ---> LED anode
  - LED cathode ---> Common ground

  Lantern LED (warm white, inside the craft-stick lantern on the lantern arm):
  - Arduino D5 ---> 220 ohm ---> LED anode
  - LED cathode ---> Common ground


6. SERVOS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  MG996R HEAD TURN SERVO (mounted at top of PVC spine):
  - Signal ---> Arduino D9
  - V+     ---> 4xAA servo rail +
  - GND    ---> Common ground
  - Drives foam head through 1/4" vertical dowel
  - Modular FSM software limits: 55-125 degrees (home = 90)
  - Competition firmware: headAway = 90, headForward = 60

  MG996R SYRINGE-DRIVE SERVO (mounted on top of plywood base):
  - Signal ---> Arduino D6
  - V+     ---> 4xAA servo rail +
  - GND    ---> Common ground
  - Drives the 60mL DRIVE syringe through a wooden dowel linkage
  - Drive syringe is horizontal, on top of the base, zip-tied down
  - Modular FSM software limits: 40-130 degrees (home = 45)
  - Competition firmware: hydroRest = 70, hydroPush = 18
  - Push angles were found by testing: 18 verified good, 15 flagged as a
    grinding risk (closer to the physical stop). 18 shipped.
    If it ever grinds, go UP (19/20/21). Lower is toward the wall.
  - Sweep step delay is 35ms/deg vs 20ms/deg on the other two servos, to give
    the fluid column time to transfer instead of shock-loading it.


6b. FLUID POWER CIRCUIT (the scored fluid power requirement)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Closed two-syringe water circuit, master/slave. No pump, no reservoir.

    [MG996R D6] --dowel--> [60mL DRIVE syringe, base]
                                   |
                          3/16" ID clear vinyl tubing
                                   |
                                   v
                           [60mL OUTPUT syringe, shoulder]
                                   |
                              dowel link
                                   |
                                   v
                           [right arm, pivots on a plain metal pin]

  Water is used because it is effectively incompressible (bulk modulus
  ~2.2 GPa). Any trapped air acts as a spring and absorbs the stroke.

  FILL ORDER (do not deviate):
    1. Submerge one syringe in water and draw the plunger up until the barrel
       is full with no bubbles. Clean water only -- soap strips the seal
       coating and causes stiction.
    2. Attach tubing to that syringe's nozzle; leave the far end open.
    3. Over a sink, push the plunger in slowly until water fills the tube too.
    4. Attach the second, empty syringe while that end is still wet.

  Realistic travel: the servo produces roughly 2 cm of reliable plunger
  stroke. That is enough for a clearly visible gesture. Repeatable and
  small beat ambitious and intermittent.

  SG90 LANTERN ARM SERVO (zip-tied to the end of the PVC shoulder crossbar):
  - Signal ---> Arduino D11
  - V+     ---> 4xAA servo rail +
  - GND    ---> Common ground
  - Blue-bodied micro servo, wires intact and powered (this one is a real
    actuator; the unpowered joint is the pointing-arm shoulder, see 6b)
  - Drives a wooden dowel arm carrying the craft-stick lantern and warm white LED
  - Modular FSM software limits: 60-125 degrees (home = 70)
  - Competition firmware: leftDown = 0, leftUp = 90


7. PHYSICAL FRAME SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - Base:       ~12" x 12" plywood, painted white, with 4 short wooden dowel feet
  - Spine:      1/2" PVC pipe rising from the base into a floor flange
  - Shoulders:  PVC T-junction at spine top, horizontal PVC crossbar,
                1/2" 90-degree elbow on the pointing-arm end
  - Head:       6" foam ball, painted burnt sienna, on a wooden dowel neck post
  - Neck post:  round wooden dowel on the MG996R servo horn
  - Right arm:  wooden dowel driven by the OUTPUT syringe plunger through a
                short dowel link; pivots on a plain metal pin through a drilled
                hole at the shoulder. No powered servo at the hinge.
  - Lantern
    arm:        wooden dowel with a craft-stick lantern frame, driven by the
                SG90 zip-tied to the end of the shoulder crossbar
  - Wiring:     Multi-color Dupont jumpers, zip-tied to PVC spine
  - Shell:      Fabric/clothing removable in <10 seconds for judging
  - Fasteners:  Hot glue for placement, black nylon zip ties for load. Hot glue
                alone does not hold a servo on PVC. 4" ties were daisy-chained
                (tail of one through the head of the next) where a single tie
                was too short to wrap pipe plus servo.

  Lengths and thicknesses beyond the base footprint and the 6" foam ball were
  not recorded during the build and are deliberately not stated here.
  Resting-position envelope the entry had to fit: 15" deep x 3' wide x 4' high.

================================================================================
NOTES
================================================================================
1. Tie down servo wires so they cannot pull out during the removable-shell reveal.
2. Confirm capacitor polarity before powering the servo rail.
3. Power on early enough for the PIR warm-up window (60s) to finish.
4. Test the full sequence after transport, not just before packing.
5. Keep both syringes and the tubing visible to judges -- the reveal is part of
   scoring, and fluid power is a separately scored category.
6. The right arm pivots on a plain metal pin through a drilled hole at the
   shoulder. The output syringe plunger pushes a short dowel link into the arm
   so it rotates around the pin and the pointing end rises. No servo or motor
   is used at the hinge.
7. Write each servo's home angle BEFORE calling attach(), or the servo snaps to
   the library default on power-up.
